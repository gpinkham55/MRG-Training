Reference Sheet A: STAMPED Hose Selection

Ask all seven every time. Missing one is how hoses fail early.

Letter Stands for Questions to ask Why
S Size Inside diameter? Overall length? Dash size for hydraulic? Measuring the old hose's ID, not OD? Undersized hose raises velocity, heat, and pressure drop; wrong length strains fittings
T Temperature Media temperature? Ambient temperature? Any heat sources nearby (steam lines, exhaust)? Heat above rating shortens hose life sharply
A Application What is it doing? Suction or discharge? Static or moving? Abrasion, crush, kinking? Routing and bend radius? Any certification needed (MSHA, FDA, DOT)? Determines construction (helix, braid, spiral, cover)
M Material / Media What flows through it? Concentration? Mixtures? Cleaning chemicals? Tube must resist the media; fittings must too
P Pressure Max working pressure? Surges or spikes? Vacuum? Working pressure must cover peaks, not averages
E Ends Fitting types, sizes, thread, orientation (for two elbows), material (steel, stainless, brass, aluminum)? Wrong end means a return trip
D Delivery When needed? Quantity? Any testing, tagging, or certification required? Sets quote, priority, and paperwork

Reference Sheet B: Hose Sizing & Dash Sizes

Industrial hose is sized by inside diameter (ID) in inches. A 1-1/2" water discharge hose has a 1.5" ID. Always measure the old hose's ID, not its OD.

Hydraulic hose uses dash sizes. The dash number is the ID in sixteenths of an inch.

Notes

  • SAE 100R5 hose is the exception: its dash size follows tube OD, so a -8 R5 is not a 1/2" ID. Check the catalog.
  • On fittings, the thread end's dash size refers to the tube it would mate with, not the hose ID. A -8 JIC thread is 3/4-16 (see Reference Sheet H).
  • Our coupling item IDs show both sizes: in F2-0808-JFS, the first 08 is the hose size and the second 08 is the thread size (see Reference Sheet O).

Length

  • Hose sold off the reel is entered in feet (FT), decimals allowed (6.5 ft).
  • Assembly overall length (OAL) runs from the sealing face of one fitting to the sealing face of the other (Reference Sheet I).

Rules of thumb

  • A male pipe thread always measures bigger than its nominal size, about 1/4" to 3/8" bigger across the common sizes. A thread that measures 1.05" is 3/4" pipe.

Reference Sheet E: Industrial Fittings & Attachment

Shank (stem) selection

  • The shank must match the hose ID and the attachment method. A shank built for bands may be too short for interlocking clamps, and vice versa.
  • Combination nipples: general purpose shank with serrations, NPT male thread; used with bands or clamps on water, air, light chemical.
  • Ground joint (steam) fittings: shank, spud, and wing nut with a ground metal-to-metal seal; used with interlocking clamps on steam service.
  • Crimp stems with ferrules: highest retention; used on industrial crimped assemblies (chemical, petroleum, hot water).

Cam and groove (camlock) type chart

B Coupler (female cam arms) × male NPT
C Coupler × hose shank
D Coupler × female NPT

Attachment methods

Method How it works Use Do not use
Preformed bands / band and buckle Steel or stainless band tightened over hose on shank; typically 2 per shank Water, air, light duty Steam; high pressure; critical chemical service
Interlocking clamps (Boss style) Two-bolt clamp halves that grip the hose and lock to the fitting collar Steam (with ground joint fittings), high pressure water and air With mismatched shanks

Steam safety rule: Steam assemblies use steam-rated hose, ground joint (or approved steam) fittings, and interlocking clamps. Never cam and groove, never bands, never worm gear clamps. Clamp bolts get re-tightened after the first heat-up cycle per the clamp maker. Steam hose design factor is 10:1; general industrial hose is typically 4:1.

Quick rules

  • Fittings must be compatible too: brass with ammonia is a problem; stainless for most chemical service.

Reference Sheet G: Hydraulic Hose (SAE Types)

Construction families

  • Braided (wire woven in a crisscross): more flexible, tighter bend radius, good for low to medium pressure.
  • Spiral (wire layers wound in alternating directions): handles high pressure and high impulse, stiffer, larger bend radius.
SAE type Reinforcement Pressure behavior Typical use
100R1 (R1AT) 1 wire braid Medium; pressure drops as size grows (about 3,000 psi at -4 to 1,000 psi at -16) General hydraulics, return lines
100R2 (R2AT) 2 wire braid Higher; about 5,000 psi at -4 to 2,250 psi at -16 Mobile and industrial pressure lines
100R12 4 spiral (heavy) 4,000 psi constant, -6 through -16 High pressure, high impulse
100R13 4 or 6 spiral 5,000 psi constant, -12 and up Heavy equipment, high impulse
100R15 4 or 6 spiral 6,000 psi constant Highest pressure severe service
100R16 1 or 2 wire braid, compact Similar to R2 range, tighter bend radius Tight routing
100R17 1 or 2 wire braid, compact 3,000 psi constant, -4 through -12 Constant 3,000 psi systems

Always use the manufacturer's catalog for the exact rated working pressure by size.

Key terms

  • Working pressure: maximum system pressure, including spikes. Hydraulic hose burst pressure is typically 4× working pressure (4:1 design factor).
  • Impulse life: number of pressure cycles the hose survives in testing. SAE braided types test to roughly 150,000 to 200,000 cycles; spiral R12, R13, R15 to 500,000. High-cycle equipment needs spiral or an impulse-rated hose.
  • Minimum bend radius: measured to the inside of the bend. Bending tighter than minimum shortens life. Keep a straight section of hose behind each fitting before the bend starts.
  • Temperature: many standard nitrile hydraulic hoses run about -40°F to +212°F; check the catalog.
  • Hose and fittings must match by manufacturer and series. Mixing brands is not approved unless the manufacturer explicitly cross-certifies.

Safety: fluid injection hazard Never run a hand along a pressurized hydraulic hose to find a leak. A pinhole jet can inject oil through skin and cause severe injury that requires emergency surgery. Use cardboard or wood to locate leaks, with the system depressurized whenever possible.

Reference Sheet H: Thread Identification

Tools: calipers, thread pitch gauge (inch and metric), seat angle gauge, thread ID chart.

Four-step method

  1. Look: Is it tapered or parallel? Is there a seat (flare, cone, O-ring face, O-ring on threads)?
  2. Measure the OD of male threads (or ID of female threads) with calipers.
  3. Count the pitch with a pitch gauge (threads per inch).
  4. Check the seat angle with a seat gauge (37°, 45°, 24°, 30°, 60°).

Reference Sheet I: Assembly & Crimping

Part 1: Reading a crimp spec chart Every chart lists, per hose type and dash size: the fitting series, the die set, the crimper setting, the target crimp diameter, and the tolerance (often about ±0.005", but use the chart's value). Some charts also list insertion depth and whether the hose needs skiving.

Part 2: Die selection and settings

  1. Match hose part number and fitting part number to the same manufacturer series on the chart.
  2. Select the die set the chart specifies for that dash size (dies are usually color or number coded).
  3. Set the crimper to the chart setting (or the ring setting on our crimper).
  4. Every crimper drifts a little. The Shop Mentor keeps a log of the actual diameters each setting produces on our crimper. If a setting consistently runs 0.004" large, the log shows the adjusted setting to use.

Part 3: Cut length and overall length (cutoff allowance)

  • Overall length (OAL) runs from the sealing end of one fitting to the sealing end of the other. For elbows, measure to the centerline of the sealing face.
  • Cutoff allowance (C/O), sometimes called drop or fitting allowance: the length a fitting adds beyond the end of the hose. Each fitting's C/O comes from the catalog.
  • Cut length = OAL − (C/O of fitting 1 + C/O of fitting 2).
  • Example (illustrative values): OAL 36.00". Fitting 1, female JIC swivel, C/O 1.25". Fitting 2, male NPT, C/O 1.50". Cut length = 36.00 − 2.75 = 33.25".
  • Standard length tolerance for most assemblies: check our shop standard with the Shop Mentor (commonly ±1/8" on short assemblies, more on long ones).

Part 4: Skive vs no-skive

  • No-skive fittings crimp directly over the hose cover. Most modern hydraulic fittings are no-skive.
  • Skive fittings require removing the cover (external skive) and sometimes part of the inner tube (internal skive) so the fitting bites the wire. Skive length comes from the chart.
  • Using a no-skive fitting on hose meant for skive fittings, or the reverse, causes blow-offs. Match the series.

Part 5: Build procedure (hydraulic, crimp style)

  1. Safety glasses on.
  2. Confirm hose and fittings from the work order and spec chart.
  3. Cut hose square on the abrasive cutoff saw.
  4. Clean the bore (projectile cleaner or air) to remove cutting debris.
  5. Skive if required.
  6. Mark insertion depth on the hose with a paint pen.
  7. Push the fitting in to the mark. Lubricate only with clean hydraulic oil if the maker allows; never grease or soap.
  8. Position the fitting in the die per the chart (ferrule fully in the die, not past the ferrule edge).
  9. Crimp.
  10. Measure (Part 6).
  11. Confirm the insertion mark is still at the ferrule edge (no push-off during crimp).
  12. Orient elbows to the specified angle before crimping the second end.
  13. Clean, cap both ends, tag with part number, date, and builder initials.
  14. Proof test if the order calls for it.

Part 6: Crimp diameter verification

  1. Use calibrated calipers or a micrometer.
  2. Measure across opposite die faces at the middle of the crimped section, not across a seam or ridge left by the die gaps.
  3. Take two readings, rotating about 90° between them.
  4. Compare to the chart target and tolerance.
Result Action
Within tolerance Pass. Record measurement on the assembly record.
Too large (under-crimped) Re-crimp at an adjusted setting, then re-measure. Retention is at risk if shipped.
Too small (over-crimped) Scrap the end. Over-crimping damages the hose tube and wire; it cannot be undone.

Example: Target 1.025", tolerance ±0.005" (pass range 1.020" to 1.030"). Readings 1.034" and 1.035": under-crimped, adjust and re-crimp. Readings 1.016" and 1.017": over-crimped, scrap.

Part 7: Proof testing

  • Proof test pressure is typically 2× rated working pressure, held for 30 to 60 seconds, per SAE J343 / ISO 6605 practice and the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Test only on the test bench. Bleed all air before pressurizing.
  • Pass: no leaks, no fitting movement, no bulges. Record pressure, hold time, result, and tester initials.

Part 8: Inspection and safety checklist

  • Hose and fittings same manufacturer and series
  • Correct die set and setting from chart
  • Crimp diameter in tolerance, two readings recorded
  • Insertion mark at ferrule edge
  • Elbow orientation correct
  • Bore clean, ends capped
  • Assembly tagged
  • Proof test record (if required)
  • No cuts, kinks, or cover damage

P×T check

  1. Multiply max operating pressure (psig) by max temperature (°F).
  2. Compare to the gasket sheet's P×T limit for that thickness, and confirm pressure and temperature each fall below their individual maximums.
  3. If the result lands in the manufacturer's "consult engineering" zone, escalate before quoting.

Example: 150 psig × 350°F = 52,500. Compare to the chart for the specific grade and thickness.

Thickness rules

  • 1/16" is preferred for CNA and PTFE on smooth, undamaged raised-face flanges: less creep, better blowout resistance.
  • 1/8" for warped, pitted, or misaligned flanges and for rubber gaskets on low pressure service.
  • Thicker is not "more seal." Thicker gaskets creep more and blow out easier.

Pulp and paper quick guide (confirm with charts)

  • White liquor (caustic): EPDM (low temp), ePTFE, or graphite
  • Black liquor: ePTFE or graphite in high temperature service
  • Chlorine dioxide (bleach plant): PTFE family
  • Steam headers: CNA (moderate) or graphite (high pressure/temperature)
  • White water and general process water: EPDM or neoprene

Reference Sheet K: Flanges, Pressure Classes & Gasket Sizes

Flange facings

  • Raised Face (RF): most common on steel flanges. Uses a ring gasket that sits inside the bolt circle.
  • Full Face (FF): sealing surface runs the full flange OD. Uses a full-face gasket with bolt holes. Common on cast iron, ductile, PVC, and FRP flanges; a ring gasket on these can crack the flange when bolted.
  • Ring Type Joint (RTJ): metal ring in a groove; outside soft gasket scope, refer to engineering.

Reference Sheet L: Sheet Yield

Square grid method (conservative)

  1. Pitch = gasket OD + spacing (use 0.125" unless the waterjet program calls for more).
  2. Pieces across = sheet width ÷ pitch, rounded down.
  3. Pieces down = sheet length ÷ pitch, rounded down.
  4. Yield = across × down.

Example 1: 4" Class 150 RF ring (OD 6.88") on a 60" × 60" sheet. Pitch = 6.88 + 0.125 = 7.005". 60 ÷ 7.005 = 8.56, round down to 8. Yield = 8 × 8 = 64 rings.

Example 2: 4" Class 150 full-face gasket (OD 9.00") on 60" × 60". Pitch = 9.125". 60 ÷ 9.125 = 6.58, round down to 6. Yield = 6 × 6 = 36 gaskets.

Nesting bonus: the center drop of a 4" Class 150 ring (ID 4.50") fits a 2" Class 150 ring (OD 4.12"). The 2" ring center (ID 2.38") fits a 1/2" ring (OD 1.88"). Nesting turns scrap into product when the customer orders mixed sizes.

Cost per piece Material cost per gasket = sheet cost ÷ yield. Add cutting time and handling. Example: if a sheet cost $480, then 480 ÷ 64 =  * *7.50 material per 4" ring** before cutting labor and margin. (Practice number; use the cost P21PLAY shows.)

Reference Sheet M: Bolt Torque & Installation

We give installation guidance, not engineered torque values. Torque values come from the gasket manufacturer's data or the customer's engineering standard. Never guess a torque number for a customer.

Installation steps

  1. Inspect flange faces: clean, no radial scratches across the sealing surface, no warping.
  2. Use a new gasket every time. Never reuse a compressed gasket.
  3. Do not use anti-seize or compounds on the gasket faces unless the gasket maker specifies it.
  4. One gasket per joint. Never stack two gaskets.
  5. Lubricate bolt threads and nut faces (affects torque accuracy).
  6. Center the gasket; ID must not hang into the bore.
  7. Hand-tighten all bolts evenly.

Tightening passes (cross/star pattern)

Pass Target
1 Snug, hand tight, star pattern
2 About 30% of final torque, star pattern
3 About 60% of final torque, star pattern
4 100% of final torque, star pattern
5+ 100%, circular (clockwise around) until nuts no longer turn

Star sequences (bolts numbered clockwise around the flange)

  • 4-bolt: 1, 3, 2, 4
  • 8-bolt: 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8
  • 12-bolt: 1, 7, 4, 10, 2, 8, 5, 11, 3, 9, 6, 12

Retorque: follow the gasket maker's guidance. Never retorque a hot, pressurized joint.

Troubleshooting a leaking flange (questions to ask the customer)

  • Was the gasket new? Right material for media and temperature (P×T)?
  • Right thickness and dimensions (ID intrusion, OD hitting bolts)?
  • Star pattern and multiple passes used?
  • Flange faces damaged or misaligned?
  • Bolts lubricated? Right grade?

Reference Sheet N: O-Rings

Sizing. Most standard O-rings follow the AS568 dash-number system. The dash number sets both inside diameter and cross-section.

AS568 series Cross-section (inch) Cross-section (fraction)
-004 to -050 0.070 about 1/16
-102 to -178 0.103 about 3/32
-201 to -284 0.139 about 9/64
-309 to -395 0.210 about 13/64
-425 to -475 0.275 about 9/32

To identify an O-ring, measure its inside diameter and cross-section (thickness), then look up the AS568 size. Metric O-rings are called out as ID x cross-section in millimeters.

Durometer. Hardness on the Shore A scale. 70A is standard; 90A resists extrusion at higher pressure.

Materials

Material Good for Avoid
Nitrile (Buna-N) Oil, hydraulic fluid, fuel, water Ozone, brake fluid, ketones
FKM (Viton type) High temperature, fuels, many chemicals Hot water, steam, ketones
EPDM Water, steam, brake fluid Petroleum oil and fuel
Silicone Wide temperature range, food contact Abrasion, dynamic seals, fuel
Neoprene Refrigerants, weather Strong acids

Cord stock. For large or odd sizes we sell O-ring cord by cross-section and length, for example a 0.750" cross-section nitrile cord in 70 durometer, cut to length and spliced.
